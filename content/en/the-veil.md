## I. Let us imagine

Imagine that there is someone who knows everything. Someone outside the game — call him what you like, God if that helps you; or the programmer, if you prefer, to whom the rules of the game do not apply. He is above them. He does not play. And that is why he knows what cannot be known in here.

Ask him whatever you want. What are we doing here — whether there is a purpose —, what consciousness is. What happens when you die: whether there is something afterwards, whether there is nothing, and what that nothing would even be like. Or ask him, since he knows everything and the future is also part of everything, what is going to happen tomorrow. Perhaps one of these has been on your mind for a long time, perhaps yours is a different one, perhaps none.

And now the question that matters, which is none of those: if he wanted to give you the answer, could he? And you, could you understand it? And most importantly: would it be of any use?

The answer to these questions is almost as complicated as the previous ones, but not impossible. So here we go.

The answer is no. And not for lack of intelligence or goodwill: but because there is something in between that stops us from getting through. A limitation woven into the very form of our mind, which makes certain things impossible to say or to think — not because they are difficult, but because they are incompatible with the instrument. A mental wall, a veil. And this essay is about that: about a limit that cannot be seen, because it is not in front of our eyes, but made of them.

But before we begin, imagine something else. An enormous door, so tall that its top cannot be seen. And in front of it, a guardian who has been there forever. Nobody has seen what lies on the other side, and about the guardian the essential thing is unknown: whether he guards the door to protect you from what is behind it, or to deprive you of it. Custodian or jailer. Keep that figure in mind: we will come back to it when we know enough to judge it.

And one last thing, which is a warning and a promise. The veil cannot be seen, or touched, or heard — there is no sense with which it comes out to meet us. But it can be felt. There is a point at which thought, in full motion, stops advancing in a straight line — not because a piece of data is missing, but because the ground runs out. That is what we are going to do here: I am not going to describe the veil to you; I am going to take you to it so that you notice it yourself. Several times. And when it happens, do not attend only to the argument. Attend to the sensation.

## II. The veil and its three forms

First, what kind of thing is a wall that is not a wall. Let us imagine a two-dimensional being. He lives on a plane: he knows length and width, and nothing more. He only experiences left and right, but not up and down. For him, volume is not something distant or something difficult: it is something impossible. He may come to intuit it mathematically — he can calculate what would happen if a third direction existed —, but understanding it, experiencing it, visualizing it, is forbidden to him, not for lack of intelligence but because of the form of his being. Volume is a wall for him. But let us look at what kind of wall: it is not a partition set in front of him, something he could go around or knock down. It is a wall made of his own structure. It does not block his view: it is the shape of his eyes.

We would be in an analogous situation, with one difference: our wall is not geometric but mental. There are aspects of reality — I suspect the most fundamental ones — that are inaccessible to us as volume is to the flat being. We can feel our way toward them by analogy, circle them with metaphors, intuit them. Understand them, no. And the proof that this limit exists is not a revelation: it is that every reflection carried through with rigor to the very end ends up running headlong into it. Always in the same place. Always in the same way.

That "same place" can be mapped. The veil presents itself to us, at least, in three forms — in which thought, when it stretches, finds the limit. Although very probably the three forms are only one, as we will see. Let us touch them one by one. You do not need to believe anything: it is enough that you do the exercises.

**The form of space.** Imagine a very big ball. Visualize it: there it is. Now imagine an outrageously big ball — the size of a planet, of a galaxy. It is harder, but there it is: the mind holds it. Now imagine an infinitely big ball. Visualize it. Really try. You will notice something strange: the intention exists — something inside you stretches toward the image —, but the image does not arrive. There is a point where the stretching continues and the reaching stops. What you have just touched is the first appearance of the veil in this essay. A limit in thought. And notice why it fails, because we will use that clue later: the infinite ball cannot be seen because, to see it, everything would have to be ball — and then there would be no place where the ball is and another where it is not, and without that contrast there is nothing to see. One cannot think outside of space. That sensation, by the way, is not new: it is exactly the one produced by asking how big the universe is. The ball is only the easy version of a question that humanity has been asking since it began looking at the sky.

**The form of time.** Another exercise, even simpler. Catch the now. This instant — not the one of the previous word nor the one of the next: this one. Close your eyes and try to catch it, put your finger on it. You will notice the same sensation as with the ball: something stretches toward it, and does not reach. When you are about to touch it, it is already before; what arrives in its place is after. The present cannot be held — and notice why, because the reason is another clue —: for there to be a "now" there has to be a before and an after to delimit it. The instant exists only by splitting itself. Chasing the present is chasing the line that separates two things.

And if you cannot hold even this instant, try stretching it backwards, to the first of all: what was there before the Big Bang? Before the universe was born? There not even physics has a language — its equations burst at the initial singularity. Which came first, the egg or the chicken? It is the pocket version of the same collision with the veil: any beginning we choose can be refuted, it seems that a beginning cannot be pinned down. We can only understand in sections. Because that is the form of the veil's time: we cannot think anything without putting it in a line. Before and after. Cause and effect. Time is not something we look at: it is the line in which we are forced to place things in order to think them.

**The form of duality.** This is the quietest and the deepest. I also suspect it is the only true form. Begin with an almost childish exercise: think of a rainbow. Where does the color red end and orange begin? At what precise point does orange stop being orange and become yellow? Who — or what — decides the cut? Are we talking about colors, or about transitions? The same goes for everything. Where does your mind begin and mine end? — because your ideas are not entirely yours: they are an amalgam of other people's thoughts, mixed and rearranged. In reality nothing ends and nothing begins: it is only a sequence. The cuts are made by the mind. And the mind cannot help but cut.

Now look at what happens when one tries to think without the cut. Everything that exists seems to be made of the same thing — the same atoms in the star and in the hand, the same energy beneath all forms. So what separates us? You are not your atoms — almost all of those that made you up years ago are gone and have been replaced, and yet something persists that is still you. A wave is not its water: the water rises and falls in place while the wave travels and remains the same wave made of different water at every instant. What persists is not the substance: it is the form, the pattern. We are, each of us, more a wave than a collection of atoms — which pushes toward unity: one sea, many waves. And a nuance here, because there is a trap: the sea we can think. We can, because the sea is not everything — it has shores, it has a bottom, it has an outside: something that is not sea. The collision with the veil awaits one rung higher: think now of the absolute totality — the whole of which you are a part, that which by definition has no outside. Try it with that. There it breaks. Now try to think it, the unity. Pure unity, without remainder. It cannot be done: the very act of thinking is already two — the one who thinks and the thing thought. And if you answer "separation is only appearance", you have just created two levels — appearance and reality — and the question reappears: those two levels, are they united or separate? The move repeats itself to infinity and the crack always reappears, intact, one turn further.

Here lives, by the way, the oldest question of all: why is there something rather than nothing? It seems a question about origin, but it is a question about the cut, about duality, and asked from within it: something, nothing. Two opposite paths have pursued it to the end: science, measuring backwards until it runs out of equations; religion, decreeing a cause that needs no cause and sealing it with faith. Different treatment — a paradox pending for one, a mystery to accept for the other —, same limit. And when the two opposite paths of a civilization end at the same limit, the limit is not in the paths, it is in the instrument of thought. The mind.

There is a way to feel this form in your own chest, and it fits in four beats, although I warn you, it is a little suffocating:

> "I exist. And if I exist, it has to be in something other, because that is the only way to exist: in some place that cannot at the same time be me — otherwise I cannot be told apart from anything and therefore I do not exist. So either I do not exist, or I am everything that exists and, therefore, I do not exist."

That back-and-forth with no exit is the collision with the third form: the one cannot be thought with an instrument that works by splitting in two. And perhaps that is why the mind does what it does: it suffers a phobia of the neutral, the undefined, the unlimited, the void — of the perfect. It cannot bear what has no cut, and so it cuts. And about the cuts it tells stories: stories about the imperfect and illusory duality.

**III. The "form" of the Mind**

And now a suspicion about the architecture, because the three forms do not seem to be at the same level. Go back to the ball: it failed because a place where the ball is and another where it is not were needed — it failed because of duality. Go back to the now: it could not be held because it needed to split into before and after — duality again. I suspect, and I leave it as a suspicion, that space and time are children of duality: two ways in which splitting-in-two unfolds — one as here-and-there, the other as before-and-after. If so, the veil would have a mother form and two daughters. Although "form" is already saying too much: the form of the veil, if it has one, is not a mental form — it is precisely that which cannot have form for us, because forms are thought with the mind, and the veil is what gives form to thinking. The form of the mind.

And here one can take the most vertiginous step of this essay, because if space and time are its children, the relationship is the reverse of the one we take for granted. The mind needs to place every limit in a place and at a moment — it is its only way of thinking a limit. But this one is not in space or in time for a deeper reason than being elusive: because it is what generates them. Its form would be exactly that: the partition of whatever we experience into places and moments that, chained one after another, produce what we call experience. It is not in any moment because it is in all of them: it is what manufactures the moment. It is not in any place because it is every place: it is what manufactures the here. To look for it inside space and time is to look for the projector inside the film.

Before closing this map, it is worth being exact about what kind of limit this is, because it is easy to confuse it with simple ignorance or lack of information. It is not that we do not know the answer, as we do not know what lies at the bottom of an ocean trench or how to cure cancer. It is that the mind, taken to its edge with all rigor, does not find a wall of ignorance that a future piece of data could knock down, but a point where the question dissolves for being badly formed. The difference is total: ignorance is cured with information; the veil is cured with nothing, because it is not a lack in the content but a property of the container. We are an apparatus of a certain architecture trying to process something that does not fit its format — and the "not fitting" is not provisional.

## III. The instrument

And with what do we strike the wall? With the mind — and the mind, in order to work, needs tools. They are worth looking at closely, because here there is something that is not usually said: it is not that our tools are poor and therefore do not reach. It is that they are made of the same stuff as the wall. They have its form.

Let us begin with the first and most our own: **language**. One word, then another, then another — and never two at once. Notice the detail, because it is exact: words do not overlap in space — each one separate from the others, distinct from the others: duality — nor in time — one after another, never together: sequence. And they cannot say anything without first splitting it into subject and object, into this and that. To think with words is to think in single file. That is why language is not merely insufficient to express the timeless and the non-dual: it is constitutively opposed to it.

So far, nothing new: philosophy has been repeating it for over a century — that the limits of my language are the limits of my world, that there are things that show themselves but cannot be said. But notice what that way of putting it carries inside. If language is what limits us, then language is the cause — and a cause can be changed. A better instrument would be enough: more exact, finer, free of the vagueness of speech. The limitation would be reduced to a problem of tools, and somewhere there would be a notation capable of crossing where words fall short.

And that notation exists, or so it seems. It is called **mathematics**. It is the language with which we have gone further than with any other: formal, precise, unambiguous. If the limitation were in speech, here we ought to feel the open air. But look at what it is made of: distinguishing — this and not that —, deducing — one step and then another —, counting — the splitting-in-two repeated up to the number. Mathematics does not escape the instrument: it is the instrument polished until it shines. And the symptoms that it does not cross either are in plain view in frontier physics: the singularities where the equations burst, the infinities that have to be removed by hand from the calculations, the prostheses — like imaginary numbers — that work without anyone fully knowing why, and the uncomfortable fact that our two best descriptions of the world, relativity and quantum theory, cannot manage to speak to each other. One can read it as pending problems. One can also — and it is my suspicion — read it as the bite marks of the veil: the exact points where the most powerful language we have bites the same wall as all the others. Only further out, and with more elegance.

So the better tool did not save us. And that inverts the suspicion: perhaps language is not the cause of the limit but its symptom. We do not think with words because language is what there is, but because the only thing there can be in here is something with the form of the veil. The veil would not be limited by language; language would be shaped by the veil. Which of the two comes first, leave it open. We will return to that doubt at the end of the chapter, and it will not end where you think.

First, the proof I like best, almost anecdotal in its sheer obviousness: **binary code**. When the mind attempts the most ambitious thing it has ever attempted — to replicate itself: first computers, now artificial intelligence —, on what does it build the replica? Go down through the layers: the programs, the programming languages, the machine's instructions — and at the very bottom, the ground on which absolutely all the intelligence a human mind has been able to produce stands: ones and zeros. Duality, once again. And below that one cannot go down: not because we have not known how, but because there is nowhere to go.

What remains is the operation that uses them all, and it is the most revealing: **understanding**. What exactly do you do when you understand something? You assign it a cause and a consequence: where it comes from, where it leads. When you cannot do that, you say you do not understand it — and it is not a manner of speaking: it is the definition. But cause and consequence are creatures of time: a before that produces an after. To understand, for us, is to put in a line. So even the very act of understanding is manufactured out of one of the three forms.

And now recover the doubt I asked you to leave open, because it has just turned into something else. I was asking whether language causes the veil or the veil causes language. Notice what I was doing in order to find out: splitting the thing in two, hanging the label of cause on one and that of effect on the other, and ordering them in time. Exactly what I have just described. To ask myself whether the veil shapes my way of understanding, I have had to use my way of understanding. And that is why the question has no answer: not because a piece of data is missing, but because the cause-effect order was not out there waiting for me to discover it — I put it there when I look. The form is the form. Both things are there, and they are as they are. Which one engendered which is a question that exists only inside the instrument that asks it.

Another turn. Did you notice it? I only wanted to know whether the culprit was language, and I have ended up touching the limit in a place where I did not expect it.

Language, mathematics, binary, understanding. Everything the mind knows how to do, made of the same thing. It is not that we have a limited instrument: we are the instrument, and the instrument has the form of the veil. That is why striking the wall with it leads nowhere — it is striking the wall with the wall.

And now that you know what your tool is made of, let us do something with it. Let us use it against the one problem that does not admit it, and see what happens.

## IV. The game of the higher being

A game. Brief — but do it for real. Go back to the someone from the beginning, the one who can answer any question, and recover yours. Imagine that he exists, that he knows the answer — yours and everyone's: what lies beyond life, what meaning everything has, the rules that would make you happier here, and even the future, which for him is a triviality. And imagine that he decides to give it to you: it reaches you, by whatever route you like — a book, a voice, a message —, the information you always wanted. One single rule: at each stop, believe the conclusion for as long as it lasts.

**First stop: it could be.** You read the information, and it adds up. It seems very coherent — it answers one thing with another, it does not contradict itself, it fits with what you see. The information seems correct: applying it makes things better. It even seems almost mathematical in the way it makes sense of everything. And notice what you feel, because it is not blind faith: you understand the logic, you would like it to be true, it comforts you that it might be. Stay there for a moment and say it: it could be true.

**Second stop: it cannot be.** Wait a moment. That information has reached you in language — written, spoken or thought —, and you have just seen what language is made of: cuts and lines. It is an instrument that only knows how to say things by splitting them in two and putting them in order. Subject to the veil. And what that someone knows is, by definition, beyond the veil, which will distort the message without remedy. So the problem is not that his message arrives incomplete: it is that in passing through here it becomes something else — it stops being what it was at the exact moment it becomes comprehensible. Give it a face: a sheep and its shepherd. The sheep may have its theories about why the shepherd moves it from one meadow to another — "he takes me to the grass: he is good"; "he takes me to the slaughterhouse: he is bad" —, and be completely wrong, because the real reason — that the shepherd is walking to the village to pay for his daughter's hospital — is not that she fails to guess it: it is that it does not fit in her. There are no hospitals in a sheep's world. That is you before the one on the outside — and worse: the sheep at least shares a meadow with her shepherd. So what reached you cannot be his message: at most, the noise of the message breaking against your format. And a final blow: if he knows it cannot reach you — and he knows: he is outside —, what kind of being sends it to you anyway? One who plays with you. There would be something malign in the very attempt. Conclusion, and it is solid: it cannot be. There is no message.

**Third stop: it could be — again.** Comfortable in the no? Look at what you built it with. "He would not understand", "he should not", "it would be malign": all those verdicts are measured with a yardstick — yours, the one from inside. And you have just accepted that this yardstick does not reach the one outside. If the sheep cannot understand the shepherd's motives, neither can she judge them: neither accuse him of playing with her, nor assert that he is not guiding her. You fabricated your accusation of bad faith with the tool you declared useless. And one more thing: you can guide an ant to some place — in a limited way, without it understanding anything. Perhaps he has a way you do not know. The sheep, in the end, follows the shepherd without knowledge: with something she mistakes for truth if she does not think about it too much — with faith. So yes: he could be telling you something, and it only seems impossible to you because you judge it from here. Look closely at what has just happened: the very argument you used to refute has just resurrected the refuted.

**Fourth stop — although more than a stop it is the ground giving way.** If your yardstick is of no use to measure anything in this terrain, neither is it of use to measure what you are concluding right now. This sentence. You cannot be sure that it is possible; nor that it is impossible; nor that you cannot be sure. The only conclusion available is that there is no conclusion — and not even that one. Stop for a second. Notice the ground moving beneath the last thing you thought. That is the veil. I have not described it to you: you have just touched it.

Step out of the game now and look at it from above. Four stops, and at each one the clear sensation of advancing — a new argument, better founded than the previous one —; and the complete journey returned you to the starting point, with emptier hands, because now you know that you cannot stay there either. It is the same thing that happened to you a moment ago with language and the veil, when you were trying to decide which caused which: there is no premise missing. Does the figure sound familiar? It is the flat being walking on a sphere: he swears he is advancing in a straight line toward an infinity that never arrives, and in reality he is going around in circles — what he calls "infinity" is the name his perception gives to a curvature he cannot see. That is exactly what your thought has just done: each stop seemed like new terrain, and it was the same curve. The spiral is not a flaw in reasoning that a better premise could fix. The spiral is the phenomenon. The veil cannot be seen head-on, because there is no front; but it can be mapped by its collisions, as the blind man maps the room by his bumps. The wall is not seen. It is heard.

If you think about it, what we have really done is chase a certainty that escapes every time you think you have it. It is something like the exercise of trying to capture the present. Like a horizon that recedes at the same speed at which you run toward it.
Any voice that claims to speak to you from outside the veil — human, divine, extraterrestrial, inner — is deactivated as an authority. Not refuted: deactivated. In a way, the veil hands you back the complete responsibility of judgment. And then what are we left with? If no voice can give it to you ready-made — what is true, what is good, how to live —, the answer points to an uncomfortable place. We will see it at the end.

## V. The one who senses the veil

You can think about your own thinking. Stay with that, because said quickly it seems like little and it is, perhaps, the strangest thing in this whole essay. Right now you can observe what you think, judge it, correct it — think about thinking. And in order to do so, thought has to split in two: thinker and thought. Only that here what is thought is the very thought with which one thinks. You have only one. There is no other from which to look — and yet, the splitting happens. It is duality applying itself to itself: the painting painting itself. Something that should not be able to happen — and that, strictly speaking, does not happen: what there is is a loop, thought turning on itself, believing it sees itself. This entire essay is that loop put in writing: the veil trying to describe the veil with material from the veil.

And from there, the question I have been dodging throughout the text: what is that, in you, that senses it? A player inside a game should not be able to ask himself whether he is in a game. The flat being should not suspect volume: his geometry does not contain the question. And here we are — you and I, from the inside, touching barriers we should not even be able to conceive. Something registers the blows. It does not understand the room, but it notices that there is one. The strangest thing of all that has been said so far is not the veil: it is that the sensation of the veil exists. Who has it?

A proposal, left open. Review the collisions of this essay: none of them touched your body. The ball, the now, the unity, the four stops of the game — all of them happened in the same organ: thought. What if thought were a sense? Not just one more: the chief sense. Sight, hearing, touch live in the brain and deliver their share there; and something gathers all of that into a permanent judgment — that which we call thinking. Each sense has its limit — there are colors the eye cannot see, frequencies the ear cannot pick up — and nobody is scandalized. If thought is the sense that stands above the others, its limit would also be above: not a missing color — the entire veil. Perhaps.

And what is it that notices that limit? I do not know, and this is the honest counterweight. There may be something peering out from behind the apparatus: something that does not entirely coincide with the machinery, that notices the projection, that misses an outside it has never seen. And there may not: the suspicion of the veil may be just another product of the veil — the most sophisticated of its folds. I cannot rule out either of the two, and you already know why: ruling out would require looking from where one cannot look. I only leave the datum, which seems to me the strangest of all those available: the sensation exists. Something, in here, asks about the outside. What we do with that is the only thing left to say.

## VI. The guardian of uncertainty

Throughout the essay I have been maintaining that a concrete truth cannot be accessed. And now I am going to offer you one. I know how it sounds — it is a contradiction, and I am not going to dodge it, because it will turn out to work in my favor. I am going to give the veil a function. This is my thesis.

And before beginning, look at the question I am asking myself, because it is already rigged. "Why is the veil there? What for?" The first asks for a cause; the second, a purpose. And you already know what both are made of: of a before that produces an after, of a means that points to an end. The ultimate, by definition, has no before that produces it — asking it for a cause is colliding again. The question about the veil is manufactured with the material of the veil. And that recurrence — falling back into the same thing just when I thought I was finally looking at it from outside — is, at bottom, the best proof that it exists. A limit that resembles none that the mind knows how to think: it is not spatial — it is not anywhere — nor temporal — it neither begins nor ends. The closest thing would be a horizon: it is imbued in the very system with which we ask, and we will never find it in front of us, because we are wearing it. So what comes next is not an answer. It is a well-built suspicion. I make do with that.

Begin with something you cannot avoid: deciding. Not sometimes — always. Not deciding is also deciding; staying still is a move; neutrality is a position; even the attitude with which you bear the condition of having to decide is another decision. There is no rest, no outside of choosing. And now ask yourself what would remain of a decision if you knew, with certainty, what to choose. Nothing: executing. Following the obvious instruction, like someone solving a sum. For choosing to be yours, certainty has to be withdrawn. The same mechanism that makes you free is the one that denies you firm ground — they are not two facts that coincide: it is a single one, with two faces.

And from deciding hangs everything else. Not knowing what is going to happen is what pushes you to act — to foresee, to work, to try to make what you want happen. Expectation against result: from that clash come the emotions. The excitement when you bet, the joy when you get it right, the disappointment when you do not, the hope while you wait. And expectation can only exist if you do not know: when you know, there is no bet — there is paperwork. That is the difference between a machine and a person; between executing and living. And here the nuance that seems to me the most important in the whole essay: it does not matter whether free will exists or not. It does not matter. The only thing that matters is that you have the sensation that it exists — because the moment that sensation disappeared, everything else would disappear: expectations, emotions, excitement, disappointment; everything that an experience encompasses.

Remember what you asked for at the beginning. If your choice was to know what was going to happen tomorrow, and it seemed to you the best possible gift, it effectively is — but only once, and only because you cannot. Imagine that you could always: every result before the match, every answer before the question, every ending before the beginning. By the third week you would return the gift. What you wanted was not the datum: it was the advantage of having it while others do not, the thrill of getting ahead. To know everything is not to win the game before playing it: it is that there is no game. The future would resemble, more than anything, a memory and, frankly, that sounds horrible.

And that uncertainty from which everything hangs has a guardian. It is the veil. You have touched it several times already: it is what prevents you from seeing everything as a whole and forces you to see it in parts, in sequence, in dual — a before and an after, a here and a there, a present that never lets itself be held. Return now to the enormous door of the beginning, and to the one standing guard in front of it. You can now read the whole scene. Behind the door is certainty: seeing everything, knowing everything. And the guardian is not there to deprive you of a treasure. He is there because, if the door were opened, everything that came before would go out at once — the expectation, the thrill, the decision, the story. Certainty is not the prize of the game: it is its end. My verdict, subjective like everything of mine: the guardian protects the experience of living. Although only perhaps, because I still cannot understand what lies behind the door. I only know what is not there.

And now the proof — the strangest an essay can offer, because it works backwards. Everything I have just asserted can be refuted. Do it: with the mind almost anything can be eroded, and this thesis is especially vulnerable — it declares itself incapable of proving itself. And what do you get by knocking it down? Another turn. Another conclusion that will not let itself be fixed. Another spin on the sphere. To refute the veil is one more move within the veil: to attack the thesis is to practice it. I cannot prove it to you; I can only make each attempt to deny it return you to it. If you have gotten this far arguing with me, and your head has not exploded, you have already verified it.

And now, one last exercise — the strangest of all, because this time you are not going to imagine a higher being who knows everything: you are going to be him. Put yourself completely in his skin. You know everything. Every answer, every ending. You know what is going to happen tomorrow and in a thousand years, and you know it with the same indifference with which you know what happened yesterday. Stay there for a moment, seriously. And now tell me: what do you want? What would move you? What could you desire, if you lack nothing and nothing can surprise you? Only one thing occurs to me. You would want, for a while, not to know. You would want to wait for something, to fear something, to be wrong, to be surprised. You would want to live — and for that there is only one way: to cover yourself with a veil. The veil would not be your condemnation: it would be the only thing he could envy you.

What remains is my own part. I did not find the veil by reasoning: I found it by colliding with it. My thought walked so far that the multiple footprints it left gradually revealed that I was retracing my own steps again and again, I was going around on a sphere that I believed to be flat. A mental sphere, on which a force like gravity keeps me clinging to the curved ground, although this force does not act on my body, it acts on my mind. I have spent my life returning to the same things, and by dint of returning I noticed the pattern. And I will tell you why I cannot stop, because it explains more than it seems. When people ask me what I want — money, a house, goals —, I never know what to answer. I always see both sides of everything: an excess of subjectivity that sounds like a virtue and that, in excess, is something else — it does not let you believe in anything, convince yourself of anything; and without conviction there is nowhere to go: the more neutral, the less energy the body puts into anything, because nothing convinces enough. My honest answer to "what do you want?" is: I want to want something. And the only thing that ignites me like that is this: chasing a horizon that I know recedes with every step I take toward it. Battling against the guardian knowing that defeating him could be the end of the life I am in — because what I pursue, certainty, would extinguish exactly everything that living is. I am not interested in obtaining it. And I cannot stop pursuing it. Another loop, yes. But before you smile, look at yourself: the one who loves is on the same wheel without seeing it — loving and suffering, loving and suffering. I only look at it head-on. And mind you, I still have one spring left, an unexpected certainty. At this point in the text, am I going to throw out a certainty? Yes: The certainty of doubt. Exactly. So, once again, I return to the ring and say: I cannot be sure that the guardian is good and that he is invincible. And this is, precisely, the fuel that keeps the flame going.

And the last turn, which by now you can see coming. I cannot be sure of any of this — not of the guardian, not of the function, not of this sentence. That I cannot be sure is, according to my own thesis, the proof that it is true; but since nothing can be true, neither can this; and that neither can this be true confirms it again — and I could go on like this to infinity, until I die, without ever finishing. That wheel that does not stop is the greatest proof I have of the veil, and of there being a guardian of an uncertainty without which there would be nothing to live. The wheel was turning while I wrote this, it turns while you read it, and it will go on turning when you close the text and return to your life. I know that not everyone will feel it — there are those who do not ask themselves these questions, and live just as well or surely better. Or perhaps worse.

Although I am not entirely sure.
